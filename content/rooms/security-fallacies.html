National security: publish the assumptions behind the bill

Assess security as a portfolio of threats, dependencies and useful resilience, with explicit failure cases and review triggers. Expensive equipment is not its own proof of safety.

Security includes whether ordinary systems keep working and whether people can repair or replace them. Distributed skills, local capability and diverse dependencies offer a wider set of questions than a purchase price.

How it could work

  1. Each supported programme records the threat, evidence, delivery schedule, dependencies and the consequence of failure.
  2. Compare concentration and diversification, including civil energy, communications, food, health and repair capability.
  3. Run independent exercises and publish non-sensitive lessons, changing investment when its assumptions no longer hold.

Everyday example

A regional hospital tests whether power, communications and supplies survive a disruption, with a named owner for fixing each failure.

Who could act and how it could be funded

National strategy belongs to the Commonwealth; states and local institutions can deliver civil resilience and procurement standards. Fund maintenance, exercises and skilled operators alongside equipment.

Fair-go safeguards

No claim that distribution automatically eliminates risk. Sensitive details receive specific protection rather than a blanket secrecy label. Civil projects are judged on their own purposes.

What would show progress

Exercises passed; dependencies reduced; recovery time; lessons acted on; service continuity; full costs.

What we can check

Defence strategies and investment programmes set out official rationales and planned capabilities. They are plans to examine, not proof that a chosen purchase will deliver its intended result. Use the publication date, delivery assumptions, costs and review mechanisms when comparing a strategy with alternatives.

Defence: strategic planning and published strategies

Choices and tradeoffs

Test military assumptionsAsk whether deterrence, readiness and delivery schedules hold under plausible scenarios. Sensitive detail may need protection, but public objectives and accountability should remain intelligible.
Invest in civil resilienceEnergy, communications, health, food, repair and community capability can keep society functioning through disruption. They also need exercises, maintenance and evidence of benefit.
Diversify relationships and dependenciesAlliances, diplomacy and interoperable systems can expand options. Concentration in one supplier, network or partner can also narrow choices; diversity carries coordination costs.

A practical public voice

Use an assumption ledger: threat, evidence, proposed capability, cost, time to deliver, dependencies, failure case and an alternative. Ask what finding would trigger a change of course. Avoid the opposite fallacy too: showing a weakness in one security plan does not establish that every alternative is safe.

Test the dependency, not the promise

The missing-middle compute proposal treats skills, local recovery and the ability to change providers as security assets. Compare concentrated and distributed capability against specific failures, costs and recovery tasks. Distribution alone does not guarantee security, and infrastructure is only one part of this wider debate.
