Promising treatment. Who can reach it?

Psychedelic-assisted care raises questions about evidence, access, cost, supervision and the kind of support that surrounds a medicine.

Open the roomAll Twinkles

What we can check

TGA’s authorised-prescriber pathway permits specifically authorised psychiatrists to access MDMA for PTSD and psilocybine for treatment-resistant depression. These remain unapproved medicines, rather than products evaluated and entered on the ARTG through the ordinary approval pathway. Permission under this pathway is not general legalisation or a take-home treatment entitlement.

Choices and tradeoffs

Supervised access with outcome reportingA regulated pathway can offer access for selected patients while recording benefit and harm. Cost, workforce and selection criteria can leave access unequal.
Public-interest research and trainingIndependent trials, longer follow-up and supervised practitioner training can clarify who benefits and under what conditions. More evidence takes time and should include negative findings.
Equitable funding and continuityEvaluate funding alongside existing effective care, preparation and follow-up. Public resources need comparative benefit, patient choice and protection against exaggerated marketing.

A practical public voice

Patients can discuss the official pathway with a qualified treating professional. For policy, ask health representatives about independent evaluation, affordability, consent, practitioner training and reporting. Personal suitability cannot be determined by a campaign page. Treatment access and plant-law reform are separate decisions.

From a research concept to a care pathway

Consciousness Parlor proposes professionally guided psychedelic research and therapy. Examine preparation, supervision, follow-up, consent and access costs against condition-specific evidence. The concept is not an operating clinic, an approved protocol or proof of effective treatment.
