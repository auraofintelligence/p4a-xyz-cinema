Twinkle 26 / Brainstorming scenario

Pay equity: visible progression and a fair share of care

Make pay, promotion and caring penalties visible in public-sector and participating employer systems. Support women-led investment and leadership through Queens while improving ordinary workers' opportunities.

500 Queens is a proposed women’s leadership and enterprise pathway, with mentoring, practical projects and a substantial source library. It adds ownership and decision-making to the question of pay and caring penalties.

Explore the connected work

  • 500 Queens 2026 — Women’s enterprise and leadership proposal, with mentoring concepts, an enterprise catalogue and original source readers. Source repository
  • 500 Queens: care and participation — Voluntary contribution and care alongside women’s leadership, learning and paid enterprise pathways. Source repository
  • C-Hour Introduction — One verified voluntary human hour equals one C-Hour, with no fixed dollar equivalent. Explore recognition, care and local choice. Source repository

How it could work

  1. Audit comparable roles, progression and leave impacts; publish aggregate findings and an accountable repair plan.
  2. Design flexible progression and shared caring arrangements that do not quietly remove people from advancement.
  3. Use women-focused leadership and investment pathways as a separate ownership lever, with their own results reported.

Everyday example

A part-time parent can see the route to promotion and whether the same work is paid consistently, rather than being told flexibility explains everything.

Who could act and how it could be funded

States can lead through public employment and purchasing; national workplace settings require Commonwealth engagement. Employers fund job evaluation and corrections; leadership pilots disclose operating and investment costs.

Fair-go safeguards

Queens is for women; it is not a programme for boys or a substitute for youth participation. Protect salary privacy and distinguish workforce composition from unequal pay for comparable work.

What would show progress

Pay and progression changes; caring penalties; retention; leadership access; repairs completed.

What we can check

WGEA’s 2024-25 private-sector total-remuneration average gender pay gap is 21.1%. This measure includes more than base salary and uses annualised full-time-equivalent pay. It does not mean every woman receives 21.1% less than a man doing the identical job. Mean, median, industry and employer measures answer different questions; compare the same measure over time.

WGEA: measures and employer data

Choices and tradeoffs

Pay transparency and job evaluationPublished ranges and consistent evaluation can reveal unjustified differences. Organisations need a correction process and protection against retaliation, while respecting personal salary information.
Progression and caring-compatible workPredictable hours, usable parental leave for all parents and promotion access can widen opportunity. Flexible arrangements fail if the career penalty simply moves out of view.
Value care work and build leadership routesReview pay, skills and advancement in care-heavy sectors and support women’s enterprise. Funding and workforce implications need an explicit public receipt.

A practical public voice

Look up your employer in WGEA’s explorer, read its explanation and ask about its action plan. Through a union, staff forum or representative, ask for comparable-role audits, promotion outcomes and leave take-up. Do not infer individual discrimination from one aggregate number.

Who gets to lead and invest?

500 Queens and Designing Equality explore women’s executive and investment decision-making pathways. That is a specific leadership and ownership lever. Judge its outcomes separately from equal pay, progression and caring penalties; a proposal is not evidence that the pay gap has closed.
