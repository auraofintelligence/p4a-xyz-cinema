Super for housing: compare the whole life, not just the deposit

Publish a transparent household comparison before supporting a super-for-housing scheme. Test retirement money, housing security and market effects together, including people with too little super to benefit.

Taking money from super changes both the housing path and retirement path. A transparent model could compare the whole household outcome with supply, tenure and shared-ownership alternatives.

How it could work

  1. Compare no withdrawal, withdrawal, collateral and shared equity using the same household, home and time period.
  2. Account for mortgage interest, rent, maintenance, transaction costs, retained super and downside cases; show every assumption.
  3. Use results to favour measures that improve secure access without simply increasing bids for the same homes.

Everyday example

Illustration only: $20,000 retained at an assumed 5% annual return becomes about $53,066 in 20 years before fees and tax. A housing path must count rent avoided and all home costs too; that figure alone does not decide it.

Who could act and how it could be funded

Super rules require Commonwealth action; states can deliver supply and tenure alternatives. An independent comparison needs modelling and review funding; public support and price effects must be budgeted separately.

Fair-go safeguards

Illustrative assumptions are not forecasts or personal advice. No guaranteed return or house-price claim. Include renters and households with small balances, and publish losses as well as gains.

What would show progress

Secure access across income groups; retirement position; total housing cost; supply added; sensitivity to downside assumptions.

Proposal, review and law are different

In the Liberal Party’s 12 August 2026 National Press Club address, super-for-housing policy was described as remaining under review. The earlier Super Home Buyer Scheme costing describes an earlier proposal, not an enacted general right to withdraw super for a deposit. Check current law and the exact scheme before treating any political announcement as an entitlement.

Compare more than the deposit

Withdraw retirement savingsMay bring ownership forward for an eligible buyer and avoid some rent. It also removes invested capital and future compounding; additional purchasing power may affect prices where supply cannot respond.
Use savings as collateralCould preserve investment exposure while helping secure finance. It links retirement security to mortgage risk and requires precise default, lender and consumer-protection rules.
Shared equityA public or other partner takes a share of the home, reducing the buyer’s initial finance need. Future gains, losses, repairs, exit and buyout obligations must be clear.
Supply and rental securityMore well-located homes and secure rental options reach people without a deposit or substantial super. Delivery takes time, construction capacity and infrastructure; tenure reform has its own landlord and tenant tradeoffs.

Show the whole household path

Compare super balance and returns, rent avoided, deposit, interest, repayments, maintenance, transaction costs, price changes and retirement housing security. Include people without a deposit or substantial super, and run downside cases. This is a policy comparison, not personal financial advice.

Ask for distribution, not just a success story

Ask your federal representative for modelling by income, age, renter status, gender and existing super balance. What happens to prices and to buyers who cannot access the scheme? Ask who bears losses and whether the same budget could house more people through supply or rental measures. Compare the final rules when published rather than carrying an old election promise forward.
