AUKUS submission / 24 August 2026

Don't put all our eggs in one basket.

The AUKUS submission asks Australia to compare its submarine investment with distributed civic capability and peaceful regional cooperation. The question is what security money makes possible, and who can use and maintain the capability it buys.

Pillar Two, beyond the arms brochureMake advanced-technology partnerships build civilian capability too: local skills, research access, open interfaces, Australian maintenance and the ability to change providers.
The missing middleSerious community computing between a phone and a giant data centre, with learning labs and locally governed workloads for science, creative work, public planning and disaster continuity.
Peace as a security investmentCompare a network of willing communities sharing knowledge, scientific work and disaster capability with the costs and dependencies of military programmes. Partners retain their own authority.

Start with the submission's proposed 32-site learning pilot, test useful work and recovery, publish the costs and failures, and compare the next investment. Its global network is a thought experiment using planning allowances, not a priced delivery contract or a promise that infrastructure alone creates peace. Civic nodes and submarines perform different jobs; the proposal asks for a serious comparison of the security each buys.
