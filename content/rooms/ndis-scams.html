Twinkle 34 / Brainstorming scenario

NDIS fraud: verify the service without punishing the participant

Give participants readable service receipts and a safe way to challenge suspicious billing. Target provider conduct while protecting continuity of essential support.

The service receipt should make a charge understandable to its participant. Useful assistance could combine accessible records, trusted advocacy and a safe dispute without interrupting care.

How it could work

  1. Providers issue accessible itemised statements showing service, date, agreed rate and delivery confirmation suited to the participant.
  2. An independent advocate can query bills; risk-based audits examine provider patterns rather than presuming participant guilt.
  3. Maintain an alternative support pathway during investigation and publish aggregate remedies and repeat-provider actions.

Everyday example

A participant can spot a billed visit that never happened and get help without losing tomorrow's essential care.

Who could act and how it could be funded

National scheme authorities and providers lead billing controls; state disability and safeguarding services can coordinate support. Fund advocacy, accessible receipts, targeted audits and continuity arrangements.

Fair-go safeguards

A participant's failure to click a confirmation is not proof that care was not delivered. Avoid surveillance, blanket freezes and automated adverse findings; preserve due process.

What would show progress

Undelivered services identified; disputes resolved; continuity maintained; participant control; audit cost and recovered funds.

What we can check

NDIA distinguishes deliberate fraud from mistakes and non-compliance. Its public guidance covers misleading claims and misuse of funds; invoices, service agreements and dates can help investigators assess a concern. An undelivered or duplicated service claim needs checking, but a billing dispute or unfamiliar disability support is not automatically fraud.

Choices and tradeoffs

Participant-readable statementsClear descriptions, dates and accessible verification can expose wrong charges early. Participants should not have to become accountants or lose service when they ask a question.
Targeted audits and provider accountabilityInvestigate suspicious patterns and preserve evidence with due process. Blanket freezes can punish legitimate participants; continuity and review need to be designed in.
Independent advocacy and supported decisionsAn independent advocate can help challenge pressure, conflicts or identity misuse. Advice must respect the participant’s choices and be funded beyond a crisis.

A practical public voice

Keep records privately and report suspected fraud through NDIA’s official form or 1800 650 717. Quality and safety concerns go to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission. Explain what you observed and distinguish suspicion from proof. Ask representatives how enforcement preserves support while recovering improper payments.
