Public choices / October 2026

The support was billed. Was it delivered?

Protect disability support from theft without turning every participant’s life into an audit. Fair enforcement and continuity of care must work together.

Open the roomAll Twinkles

What we can check

NDIA distinguishes deliberate fraud from mistakes and non-compliance. Its public guidance covers misleading claims and misuse of funds; invoices, service agreements and dates can help investigators assess a concern. An undelivered or duplicated service claim needs checking, but a billing dispute or unfamiliar disability support is not automatically fraud.

Choices and tradeoffs

Participant-readable statementsClear descriptions, dates and accessible verification can expose wrong charges early. Participants should not have to become accountants or lose service when they ask a question.
Targeted audits and provider accountabilityInvestigate suspicious patterns and preserve evidence with due process. Blanket freezes can punish legitimate participants; continuity and review need to be designed in.
Independent advocacy and supported decisionsAn independent advocate can help challenge pressure, conflicts or identity misuse. Advice must respect the participant’s choices and be funded beyond a crisis.

A practical public voice

Keep records privately and report suspected fraud through NDIA’s official form or 1800 650 717. Quality and safety concerns go to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission. Explain what you observed and distinguish suspicion from proof. Ask representatives how enforcement preserves support while recovering improper payments.

Luke’s related solutions

Luke’s Civic Ledger can inspire clear service receipts and accountable public spending, with private participant records kept out of public view. The Sovereignty Stack adds purpose limits, access records and correction rights.
