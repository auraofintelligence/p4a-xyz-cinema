The draught freeze is law

The Excise Tariff Amendment (Draught Beer) Act 2026 received assent on 8 April 2026. It sets the indexation factor to one for specified draught-beer rates on 1 August 2025, 1 February 2026, 1 August 2026 and 1 February 2027. The old “every February and August” line is therefore stale for those rates. It does not describe every packaged beer, spirit or wine tax.

The price of a drink has several levers

Targeted hospitality reliefA draught-specific concession can support on-premise trade. Its fiscal cost and actual benefit to venues, staff and customers need measurement.
Review consistency across alcohol taxesCompare beverage categories, alcohol content and administrative complexity. Reform creates winners and losers; transition and health effects need to be explicit.
Fund harm reduction and treatmentServices, prevention and late-night transport address costs not shown on a menu. They need reliable funding and evidence, rather than assuming price policy does every job.

Show the pub receipt

Ask what changed in tax, wholesale costs, rent, wages and the menu price over the same period. Luke’s Civic Ledger proposal can make the public cost and review date visible without pretending a venue’s entire bill is tax. Ask representatives how any concession’s pass-through and health effects will be evaluated.
