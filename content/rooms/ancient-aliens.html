Proposed ministerial portfolio

Australia should take the lead.

P4A proposes a Minister for UAP/NHI Disclosure and Investigation: a named responsibility for Australian records, encounters and transparent civilian investigation. The ambition is to do better than the US through honesty and a fair go at transparency, beginning with our own backyard.

UAP means unidentified anomalous phenomena; NHI means non-human intelligence. ET means extra-terrestrial, from beyond Earth. CT means crypto-terrestrial: the possibility of an intelligence hidden on or within Earth.

Australian recordsThe proposed portfolio would bring together the history of reports and government responses, seeking lawful access and release, with plain explanations of what is known, missing or still withheld.
Civilian investigationIt would support people reporting encounters and independent scientific work across land, air and sea. Original observations, methods, findings and unresolved questions would form the public work, with witness privacy respected.
Public accountabilityThe proposed minister would answer parliamentary questions and explain the work, costs and decisions.

Religion / encounters / interpretation

Gods, angels or visitors?

Call it a flying saucer and people laugh; call it a fiery chariot and it gets a stained-glass window. Why does an ancient arrival from the sky get a different reception from a modern UFO report?

Here is a premise worth exploring: what if some, or even all, of the gods of antiquity were encounters with non-human intelligences, understood through the language and beliefs of their time? What if some angels, described as heavenly messengers or attendants, were visitors or intermediaries? Revisit the accounts with modern knowledge and level heads: compare the words, the setting and the possible explanations.

Faith matters, but it is not an excuse to switch your brain off and discount rational inquiry. An inherited claim deserves scrutiny, and a new alien explanation deserves it too.

Ezekiel's wheels with eyes.

Ezekiel was a priest among the Judean exiles in Babylon in the sixth century BCE. His biblical book begins with a vision: storm, cloud, fire and four winged living creatures. In Ezekiel 1:15-21, wheels move and rise with the creatures; verse 16 describes a wheel within a wheel, and verse 18 places eyes around their rims. Above them is a throne and a human-like figure (1:26-28).

Ezekiel 10:9-17 returns to the wheels and eyes. Verse 20 identifies the creatures as cherubim, heavenly beings associated with God's presence. The text calls the experience a vision of God (1:1) and describes the appearance of divine glory (1:28).

A divine encounterRead the vision as an encounter with God and heavenly beings. What does the throne, movement and voice communicate about power and presence? How does that reading understand the eyes and the creatures?
A symbolic throneRead the imagery through the world of temples, kingship and exile. Yale scholar Christine Hayes explains the mobile throne as God's presence accompanying an exiled people, drawing on ancient Near Eastern imagery. This reading asks what the vision means in that historical setting.
Something engineered?Read the wheels as moving assemblies, the eyes as possible sensors, the brightness as an energy source, and the figure as an occupant. Could angels be perceived operators or intermediaries? These are proposed mechanical or ET correspondences: what details support them, what resists them, and what independent trace would distinguish them from metaphor?

A useful comparison keeps the actual verses, translation choices and each interpretation beside one another. Would a different translation change the imagined mechanism? Does a reconstruction explain the whole description, including the faces and wings, or only the parts that resemble modern equipment? What would change a religious, historical or ancient-alien reader's mind?

Belief / propaganda / public power

Who speaks for the gods?

A spectacular entrance is not a licence to run the country. Even if a visitor turned out to be a god or an alien, would that make every instruction a good idea? Who interprets the message, who benefits, and what happens to someone who says no?

Fear, apocalypse and death.

A prediction of the end of the world raises an awkward question: why does the leader need your savings before it happens? More seriously, when does religious propaganda turn fear into obedience or outsiders into enemies? If salvation is made conditional on death, martyrdom or violence, who is making that demand and are people free to refuse?

Jonestown gives these questions a concrete history. On 18 November 1978, more than 900 people, including children, died in the Peoples Temple settlement in Guyana under Jim Jones's leadership. The FBI's account records allegations of abuse and control, people asking to leave, and the killing of visiting congressman Leo Ryan and others.

And what about AI gods? A claim to superintelligence raises the same question about authority: who gets to choose the future? Explore AI p(doom), protopia, a fair go and Joyful Responsible Abundance.

Australian policy: inquiry and freedom.

Australia should protect freedom to believe, question, change belief and leave a group. The Australian Human Rights Commission's guide to Article 18 of the international civil and political rights covenant covers religious, non-religious and atheist beliefs, alongside freedom from coercion.

The proposed inquiry programme would put source texts and competing interpretations in public view. Australians should be free to compare religious, historical and alien readings, while public funding and decisions are explained through evidence and public purpose. Ask who benefits, what is spent and how complaints are handled.

People leaving coercive groups should have independent support and safe reporting. Threats, exploitation, pressure on children and demands for self-destruction are concrete matters for scrutiny, whatever authority a leader claims.

Bring a passage and its competing readings to a public discussion, or take a specific funding or accountability question to a representative. The Empty Chair adds another question: whose interests are absent when someone claims authority over others? The Indigenous-led partnership below develops its comparisons through each participating community's own terms and knowledge holders.

A proposed philosophy and protocol

The Empty Chair.

P4A proposes the Empty Chair as a way to consider possible non-human perspectives in human decisions. The figurative seat represents those whose identity, intentions and interests we do not know. Its purpose is empathy, black swan preparedness, and existential threat avoidance.

We act as if crypto-terrestrial and extra-terrestrial observation or interference exists: we are not alone. We make no assumption about who or what they are. Sightings and ancient traditions deserve enough seriousness to leave an empty seat.

Could an unknown presence be interdimensional? As a thought experiment, crypto-terrestrials might arrive through a portal into a cave or a “stargate temple”, rather than travel here through space. An emergence there could give the impression of local residence while showing only an arrival point, leaving origin and identity open. What observations could help Australian investigators distinguish those possibilities?

In a discussion or preparedness exercise, ask: whose perspective might be missing? Which assumptions could fail? Could a decision cause irreversible harm if we are wrong?

The chair carries no representative, vote or veto.

There's no place in this party for belittling people who take UAPs, non-human intelligence or ancient traditions seriously; questions and disagreement are welcome, ridicule isn't.

Our own documentary trail

Australian encounters, on record.

Newspaper reports, police files and government correspondence give Australia plenty to work through. These four starting points preserve what people reported and how institutions responded.

Port Augusta · 1947

The Advertiser, 8 February: railway workers reported five egg-shaped objects moving in formation. Government astronomer G. F. Dodwell treated the report as a mystery. What observations supported the shape, formation and timing?

A confidential report dated 24 July records lights observed on 15 July by several people. The balloon officer found the site’s balloons intact; inquiries checked whether a firing could explain the sighting. The file preserves differing duration estimates as well as the checks made.

Northern Territory / reported encounters

NT News, on the record.

ABC's Media Watch archive of 29 September 2008 preserves several separate NT News reports: a Marlinja encounter published on 27 June, the 23 August UFO-armada headline, and a 24 September report involving six witnesses at an outback station.

The archive includes Janie Dixon's confirmation that the Marlinja account accurately described her experience. These dated reports provide leads for finding original coverage and willing witnesses, with each account kept in its own time and place.

Pine Gap / military records

What is in the public record?

Pine Gap: the stated role

In his parliamentary statement of 22 November 1988, Bob Hawke described Pine Gap as a satellite ground station collecting intelligence for Australia and the US, including arms-control verification. Defence's 9 February 2023 statement describes the joint facilities' intelligence, communications, treaty-monitoring and missile-warning roles, and Australia's policy of full knowledge and concurrence.

Those are accounts of defence functions. A claim about a UAP encounter or an ET connection at Pine Gap needs its own dated witness account or record to examine. A useful public question is whether a named incident generated a report, which agency holds it and what can be released.

Nowra: pilot and radar, 1954

The National Archives recounts Navy pilot Lieutenant J. A. O'Farrell's report of two lights near his Sea Fury on 31 August 1954. Its account says Nowra confirmed a radar return and that the pilot's report and radar operator's confirmation are in the record. This is a specific military encounter to revisit alongside the Maralinga report above.

The Archives also records that the RAAF ceased investigating UFO sightings in 1994. The proposed civilian programme would seek surviving files and compare witness reports, instrument records and the explanations considered at the time.

Dreaming is living knowledge.

Australia has many Dreaming and Dreamtime traditions, with names and meanings specific to Country. Knowledge travels through oral histories, song, dance, art, ceremony and relationships between generations.

Sky ancestors, beings within Country and accounts of creation, change and survival deserve serious attention in their own terms. The following public accounts offer entry points through named knowledge holders and community organisations.

Proposed Indigenous-led national partnership

Sky Country, deep Country, shared inquiry.

P4A proposes a comprehensive partnership with Indigenous Australians to catalogue and compare Sky Country and underground or underworld Dreaming accounts that knowledge holders choose to share. The ambition is national: invite participation across Country and language groups, fund local teams and build a connected catalogue from community-held collections.

Participating Elders, knowledge holders and Indigenous organisations would lead the programme, set its questions and decide how accounts are described. Funding would support paid cultural work, language expertise, recording and community archives. Oral histories, song, dance and art would sit alongside written sources, with links back to the people and places that give them meaning.

Keep the account and its permissions together.

Each entry would carry its approved attribution, Country and language, original words and reviewed translation, storyteller or source, recording date and story variants. Communities would control storage, access, reuse, corrections and withdrawal. Public browsing would include only material cleared for that purpose; restricted knowledge would remain under its custodians' control.

Compare without losing the differences.

Knowledge holders would co-design categories such as sky and celestial relationships; underground, cave and water relationships; journeys, arrivals and departures; encounters and responsibilities; seasonal change, warnings and survival. The catalogue would support overlapping categories and local terms. Each tradition would retain its own account and interpretation, with similarities and differences recorded side by side.

A comparison could lead to a specific question, an agreed research project or a preparedness exercise. For example: does an approved account suggest a change in water, weather or landscape to investigate? How should a community respond to an unfamiliar encounter? Which people, places or possible presences are missing from an Empty Chair discussion? Participants would decide which comparisons are meaningful and which explanations merit further inquiry.

The first step would be funded discussions with interested communities to agree governance, benefits and a small set of voluntary pilot collections. Communities would then review the results before widening participation. The AIATSIS research framework offers a starting point for Indigenous leadership and accountability; local agreements would define the work.

Australian responsibility / regional relationships

Our region. Shared questions.

The proposed remit includes Australian records and encounters across the mainland and external territories, including Christmas Island, the Cocos (Keeling) Islands, Norfolk Island and the Coral Sea Islands. Investigation would work within Australia's lawful jurisdiction and with the agencies and communities already responsible for those places.

Across Asia, Oceania and the Indian Ocean, the proposal is cooperation: willing partners comparing encounter reports, research and safety concerns. Each partner would retain authority over its investigations and records.

Antarctic interests belong in the remit too. Australia administers the Australian Antarctic Territory under Australian law, but Antarctic sovereignty claims are not universally recognised. The Antarctic Treaty preserves differing positions on claims and provides for peaceful purposes, scientific investigation and cooperation. Any proposed work there would sit within that framework, alongside the existing Australian Antarctic program and international partners.

Proposed disclosure protections

Protect the person who brings the evidence.

A secure route, with someone on your side.

A UAP/NHI inquiry should offer confidential reporting to an independent, security-cleared team, funded independent legal advice before disclosure, protected identity records and enforceable remedies for retaliation. Witnesses should receive a written explanation of their protections, permitted routes and limits before handing over sensitive material.

P4A proposes statutory immunity from civil, criminal and employment penalties for qualifying public-interest disclosures made in good faith, on reasonable grounds, through that authorised process. Protection should cover an honest account that investigation does not substantiate. An independent reviewer should decide disputed eligibility. Deliberate fabrication and unrelated serious wrongdoing should remain accountable; reporting an offence should not erase responsibility for committing it.

Extradition safeguards should be explicit.

The proposed reform should add an express ground to refuse extradition sought to punish a qualifying public-interest disclosure, with funded representation, independent review and examination of disguised retaliatory charges. Australia should pursue compatible treaty safeguards with partner countries. Protection within Australia would not bind a foreign court or guarantee safety overseas.

The tradeoff deserves public scrutiny: witnesses need confidence to report, while investigators need to protect people, genuine operational secrets and evidence of separate serious offences. Parliament should define the protected conduct and review tests publicly, with witness advocates, lawyers, intelligence oversight bodies and civil society at the table.

What the law provides today.

As checked on 4 October 2026, the Commonwealth Public Interest Disclosure Act protects eligible disclosures of suspected wrongdoing through specified routes. It does not give everyone a licence to publish classified material, and it does not immunise a discloser's own wrongdoing. Intelligence information has additional restrictions. IGIS advises people to contact it before providing classified information so secure arrangements can be made.

Australia's Extradition Act and applicable treaties govern surrender requests. Section 7 includes political-offence and specified discriminatory-prosecution objections, subject to the Act's definitions and arrangements. These are not a general whistleblower exemption.
