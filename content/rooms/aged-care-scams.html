Twinkle 36 / Brainstorming scenario

Aged care: the receipt must match the visit

Make provider bills, delivered care and complaints understandable to the older person. Separate impersonation, financial abuse and undelivered service, with a response suited to each.

An older person’s records and chosen support could help compare the bill with the care received. They would keep a voice in the process, rather than disappear behind provider or family decisions.

How it could work

  1. Issue accessible service statements and a supported way for the person to question missing visits or unexpected charges.
  2. Provide independent advocacy chosen by the older person, including someone outside the family.
  3. Investigate repeated provider failures while maintaining essential care and recording the remedy.

Everyday example

An older person can see that Monday's visit was billed but did not happen, ask an advocate to query it and still get Tuesday's care.

Who could act and how it could be funded

National aged-care funding and oversight need provider action; states and local services support relevant safeguarding and access. Budget for advocacy, statements, targeted audits and backup care.

Fair-go safeguards

Family involvement requires the person's consent or an appropriate lawful basis; family control is not automatically safe. An allegation is investigated rather than treated as a verdict.

What would show progress

Delivered versus billed care; complaint time; continuity; remedy completed; older person's voice and control.

What we can check

My Aged Care warns about scams targeting older people and offers an official complaints route. A caller pretending to assess eligibility is different from a provider charging for an undelivered service, and both differ from coercive control of someone’s money. Suspicion should be documented and assessed; it is not a verdict on all care providers.

Choices and tradeoffs

Verify identity and make bills readableIndependent callback routes and itemised statements help people check what happened. Accessible formats, translation and trusted support are essential.
Investigate providers and protect continuityTargeted audits and incident reporting can identify misconduct. A complaint should not leave a person without care or allow an allegation to replace fair investigation.
Independent advocacy and supported decisionsProvide help chosen by the older person, including outside the family. Automatic family control can itself undermine autonomy or conceal coercion.

A practical public voice

Verify an unexpected caller through the official My Aged Care website or your known provider number. For money lost, contact the bank promptly; for care concerns use My Aged Care or the Commission’s complaint pathway. Ask how you can complain safely and keep services running. In immediate danger call 000.
