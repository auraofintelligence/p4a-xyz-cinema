Gambling harm reaches partners, children, work and mental health. Public choices include product design, advertising, access to money and support beyond a warning message.

What we can check

AIHW’s gambling overview draws on different surveys and measures of participation and risk. Those measures are not interchangeable with industry losses, a clinical diagnosis or harm to family members. Compare the population, reference period and method before using a headline percentage.

Choices and tradeoffs

Reduce advertising and inducementsCan lower exposure and pressure, especially around sport. Rules need enforceable definitions and consideration of displaced advertising and sponsorship revenue.
Binding limits and safer product designLoss or time limits and self-exclusion can interrupt harmful patterns. Privacy, evasion, coverage across providers and accessible account recovery matter.
Independent support and revenue reformTreatment, financial counselling and family support need sustained funding. Governments and communities should examine dependence on gambling revenue rather than assume it is free money.

A practical public voice

For help, use Gambling Help Online and BetStop. For policy, ask state and federal representatives which products and advertising they regulate, what outcomes they measure and how revenue conflicts are managed. You can advocate without disclosing debts or a family member’s history.
