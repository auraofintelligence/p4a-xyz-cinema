Family violence: intelligent safety and a one-stop exit

An opt-in safety pathway could join AI-assisted smart-device recording and alerting, specialist phone checks for stalking software, and one-stop support for immediate exit. A contact through 1800RESPECT could connect a person to the proposed technology-support pathway and a human who helps organise safety, transport, housing and care.

The proposed technology support belongs inside a human safety pathway. Recording, alerts and device checks would serve the person’s chosen exit plan, with one contact joining immediate help to a safe destination.

1800RESPECT: device safety

How it could work

  1. A specialist could agree safe communications and offer a consented, remotely assisted phone assessment where technically possible, with in-person or replacement-device help where needed.
  2. Chosen devices could securely record relevant incidents and send agreed alerts to trusted responders. The person and specialist would decide triggers, access and how to preserve evidence.
  3. One coordinator could arrange immediate safe exit, transport, accommodation, money, documents, children’s and pets’ needs, legal assistance and continuing housing without repeated referrals.

Everyday example

One safe contact could lead to a technology-safety assessment and an exit plan with an actual destination, instead of a person organising every piece alone while being monitored.

Who could act and how it could be funded

Specialist violence services, qualified technology teams, housing providers and relevant agencies could develop the pathway. The budget would cover secure tools, trained people, safe devices, immediate transport and continuing accommodation.

Fair-go safeguards

The person controls participation and sharing. A safety plan comes before device changes that might alert an abuser. Scans cannot guarantee detection. Recording design needs jurisdiction-specific legal review; human assessment controls alerts and escalation.

What would show progress

Time to safe exit; completed technology assessments; safe handovers; housing retained; useful evidence; survivor control and safety.

Violence against women: safety and a future

Safety, housing, legal help and recovery need to connect. AIHW’s family, domestic and sexual violence evidence separates different forms of harm and reporting sources; a dated survey measure is not an annual 2026 rate. No one needs to disclose their experience to participate in public debate.

AIHW: evidence and measures1800RESPECT: support
Safe housing and funded supportEmergency shelter and longer-term security make leaving safer. Capacity, accessibility and continuity must extend beyond one night.
Accountability and legal assistanceOrders, investigation, legal support and behaviour-change responses need timely enforcement and evaluation. A document alone does not guarantee safety.
Prevention and recoverySchools, workplaces, community leadership and trauma-informed care can address patterns before and after harm. Sustained funding and outcome measures matter more than a one-off campaign.

Safe accommodation, specialist support and timely legal help need to connect in practice. Ask representatives about local refuge access, time to legal support and continuity of care. Use private official support routes for personal incidents; this site does not collect identifiable allegations. In immediate danger call 000. The gender pay gap is a distinct topic.
