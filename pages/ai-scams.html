Public choices / October 2026

It sounded like someone you trust.

A familiar voice, polished document or convincing video can be manufactured. The response needs safer platforms and payments as well as a chance to stop and check.

Open the roomAll Twinkles

What we can check

Scamwatch describes AI-enabled impersonation, voice cloning, false endorsements, fake sites and personalised messages. Visual polish or a matching voice does not establish identity. Verification through an independently found official number or a known contact is more useful than trying to spot every visual flaw.

Scamwatch: AI and impersonation

Choices and tradeoffs

Platform and advertiser accountabilityVerified advertisers, faster takedowns and repeat-offender controls can interrupt distribution. Appeals and transparent error reporting are needed when legitimate speech or businesses are caught.
Payment checks and accessible disputesBetter warnings, trusted-contact options and recovery processes can reduce losses. Delays and false positives have costs; no page should promise automatic reimbursement.
Practical verification and supportTeach independent callback and trusted-source checks without blaming victims. Public education cannot carry the entire burden of sophisticated organised fraud.

A practical public voice

If money or account details are at risk, contact your bank through its official route promptly. Use Scamwatch to report and follow its recovery guidance. Keep evidence privately and beware of a second scam promising to recover funds for a fee. For policy, ask who must prevent, respond and resolve disputes across the platform-telecom-payment chain.

Recognising an impersonation

The AI Trust Index Australia guide describes AI-voice and fake-video scams. Use it as a literacy resource alongside the official reporting and recovery routes above; it is not an authentication service or scam detector.
