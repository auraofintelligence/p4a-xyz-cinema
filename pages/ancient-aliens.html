A working stance

The Empty Chair policy

We act as if crypto-terrestrial and extra-terrestrial observation or interference exists: we are not alone. We make no assumption about who or what they are. Sightings and ancient traditions deserve enough seriousness to leave an empty seat.

This is the workbench’s explicit premise for inquiry and decision-making, not a claim that the identity or origin of an encounter has been established.

EmpathyWhose perspective might be absent from this decision?
Black swan preparednessWhich assumptions would fail if something outside our current understanding appeared?
Existential threat avoidanceCould this choice cause irreversible harm if we are wrong? What keeps options open?

The practice is for empathy, black swan preparedness, and existential threat avoidance.

The chair marks a possible silent stakeholder. It gives no person or AI a mandate to speak for unknown beings, and grants no vote or veto.

There’s no place in this party for belittling people who take UAPs, non-human intelligence or ancient traditions seriously; questions and disagreement are welcome, ridicule isn’t.

Where the practice comes from

Cosmic Nexus’s public Extra Chair method asks who is absent but affected, what needs to wait and what should be recorded. The practice here keeps the stakeholder’s identity open.

Government records / public accountability

Show us what you have.

Official investigations and military encounter records exist. The useful demand is concrete: release the original records, explain what remains withheld, and show the evidence behind an official explanation.

Navy recordings · 2020 releaseThe US Department of Defense released three Navy videos recorded in 2004 and 2015. Its statement confirmed their origin and described the observed phenomena as unidentified. Authentic footage and a settled explanation are different questions.
A named program · 2020The Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force was established on 4 August 2020 to investigate incursions and improve understanding. Its existence is documented; ask for its records, methods and findings.
Testimony on the record · 2023–24The July 2023 House hearing heard Ryan Graves, David Fravor and David Grusch. A further hearing followed in November 2024. Read what each witness personally observed, what they were told and which underlying records can be examined.
An archive still growing · 2026NARA’s Record Group 615 now lists material from agencies including the FAA, ODNI, NSA, FBI and Air Force. Transfers are rolling; a release is a starting point for inspection, not a declaration that the public record is complete.

Australia / follow the contemporary record

It was in the paper.

Australian sightings have a long documentary trail. Start with the report made at the time, then put later explanations and recollections beside it.

Port Augusta · 1947

The Advertiser, 8 February: railway workers reported five egg-shaped objects moving in formation. Government astronomer G. F. Dodwell treated the report as a mystery. What observations supported the shape, formation and timing?

Wewak / Maralinga · 1960

A confidential report dated 24 July records lights observed on 15 July by several people. The balloon officer found the site’s balloons intact; inquiries checked whether a firing could explain the sighting. The file preserves differing duration estimates as well as the checks made.

Sky descent / earth emergence / survival / renewal

Why do these patterns recur?

People in different parts of the world tell of sky ancestors, emergence from underground, warnings of catastrophe and beings who help shape life. The comparison is worth making. Start with the named accounts and compare what they actually say.

Sky Country · Trouwerner

Aunty Patsy Cameron’s Sky Country, illustrated by Lisa Kennedy and published in 2024, shares an ancestral account of two beings descending from the Milky Way to shape the Tasmanian landscape.

Compare: descent from the sky, landscape formation and ancestral memory.

The author’s published account

Sky Woman · Oneida

In the Oneida Nation’s account told by Amos Christjohn, a woman falls from Sky World. Birds bring her safely to a turtle’s back; animals seek earth beneath the water, and the land grows.

Compare: sky origin, rescue across worlds and life made through cooperation.

Oneida Nation: Creation Story

Emergence · Oglala Lakota

The Cheyenne Creek version told by Wilmer Mesteth and retold by Sina Bear Eagle begins with people living underground in a spirit lodge while the surface world is prepared. Wind Cave connects the worlds.

Compare: underground origin, a passage between worlds and the consequences of emerging.

Named account and oral recitation

Sky and earth · Ngāti Raukawa

Te Ara presents Hūkiki Te Ahukaramū’s nineteenth-century account of Ranginui and Papatūānuku. Their separation opens a world of light for their children.

Compare: sky and earth as ancestors, darkness, separation and the conditions for life.

Named Māori account

Flood warning · Sumer

In the surviving Sumerian flood text, Zi-ud-sura hears a warning of destruction. A great boat survives the storm; the damaged text leaves parts of the sequence missing.

Compare: a warning, catastrophe, preservation and a changed world afterwards.

Manu and the fish · Vedic India

In Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa 1.8.1, Manu protects a fish that warns him of a flood. He prepares a ship, and the fish guides it to a mountain.

Compare: reciprocal care, advance warning, practical preparation and survival.

Compare an ancient-visitor interpretation

An ancient-visitor reading asks whether some accounts remember encounters with non-human intelligence. Other readings explore ancestral relationships, knowledge carried through catastrophe, contact between cultures or recurring human experiences. Put those readings beside the actual account: who arrives, what changes, what is taught, and what survives?

Look for both close correspondences and awkward differences. Record the earliest available version, translation choices and a community’s own interpretation. Similar motifs are the beginning of a comparison; the common cause remains a question to investigate.

Publicly shared knowledge / living cultural records

The rock art has a voice.

Wardaman Lightning BrothersBill Yidumduma Harney’s public account describes Jabirringgi and Yagjagula as sky-connected beings who bring rain and lightning, represented in rock art and linked with stars. It joins image, weather, Country and continuing knowledge—not just a shape cut out of a photograph.
Wandjina traditionsMowanjum’s Worrorra, Ngarinyin and Wunambal communities publicly explain the Wandjina tradition and its continuing expression through art, song and dance. Start with their account when comparing the striking imagery with a modern ancient-visitor interpretation.

Turn curiosity into a public question

What should disclosure require?

A public record indexList the incident, record holder, dates and status: released, redacted, withheld or not located. An index makes gaps visible; it still needs funding, consistent identifiers and ongoing updates.
Reasons and review datesGive a specific reason for withholding material and a route to review. Protect genuine sensor capabilities and personal details through narrow redactions rather than an unexplained blanket refusal.
Independent accessAllow qualified review of underlying records when public release is limited. Publish the reviewer’s remit, access restrictions and disagreements so an assurance can itself be examined.
Witnesses who can be heardProvide safe reporting and preserve original accounts. A hearing can expose contradictions and identify missing records; testimony still needs follow-up, not just another news cycle.

Choose one case. Ask the archive or responsible agency for its record identifier, original media, sensor data, analysis and release decision. If a file is missing, ask which locations and systems were searched. If an explanation is offered, ask which evidence supports it and what remains unresolved.

For Australian public participation, request access through the archive or the relevant agency’s information-access process, or ask a representative to seek a dated answer. Keep a public trail of the request, response, reasons and follow-up without exposing private witnesses.
