Divorce and households: affordable exits and clear agreements

Make separation processes more understandable and affordable, and develop optional household agreements for people who want to share care and resources. Safe exit and individual autonomy come first.

A shared-household agreement would make care, property, consent and exit understandable. Global Group Marriages explores voluntary adult household forms; it does not require everybody to live the same way.

How it could work

  1. Provide an accessible first appointment to map legal, financial, housing and care needs, with a plain cost schedule.
  2. Use suitable dispute resolution and supported document preparation where safe; provide independent legal help where power is unequal.
  3. Develop model shared-household agreements covering contribution, care, property, consent and exit, without claiming they create new legal marriage rights.

Everyday example

A separating parent can see the next step and likely costs; a voluntary shared household agrees what happens if a member leaves.

Who could act and how it could be funded

Commonwealth family-law reform needs coordination with state legal assistance and housing. Fund independent advice and navigation; publish the cost and limits of any subsidised process.

Fair-go safeguards

Mediation is not imposed where unsafe. No household agreement overrides a child's rights or removes access to independent advice. New legal recognition is a separate proposal.

What would show progress

Process time and out-of-pocket cost; safe housing; informed agreements; disputes resolved; workable exits.

What we can check

Australia’s Marriage Act defines marriage as a union of two people, exclusive of all others. Divorce under the Family Law Act is distinct from resolving parenting or property. No-fault divorce does not promise an automatic equal property split or an automatic parenting arrangement. Australia atlas distinguishes present legal building blocks from a new multi-adult civil status.

Choices and tradeoffs

Make existing separation processes fairerAffordable advice, timely decisions and clear financial disclosure can reduce fear and conflict. Safety screening and children’s interests must remain central; mediation is not appropriate in every case.
Recognise care and plan shared resourcesExplore co-ownership, wills, parenting responsibilities and household agreements within current law with independent advice. Private agreements do not automatically create marriage status or override statutory rights.
Debate a multi-adult recognition frameworkThe framework proposes an additional family form that shares love, care, income and risk while preserving individual sovereignty. Reform would need clear entry, voice, property, parentage and exit rules, with safeguards against pressure.

A practical public voice

Men’s concerns about cost, time with children and process deserve a fair hearing alongside women’s experiences, financial dependence, safety and diverse families. Test claims against evidence rather than assume divorce law causes reluctance to become a parent. For personal disputes, use qualified local advice; for reform, identify the exact rule and the change sought.

Household agreements and a wider thought experiment

Global Group Marriages designs the household in layers: people, love, care, resources, voice and change. Its Private Household Lab creates a local Markdown conversation sketch. U.N. of Love takes a precise comparison further: self-sovereign adult spouses across cultures, sharing romantic and sexual relationships and raising children, with a Family Assembly, Council of Care and Chamber of Repair. It is a marriage-based simulacrum beside the UN, not an operating UN institution or a replacement for it.
