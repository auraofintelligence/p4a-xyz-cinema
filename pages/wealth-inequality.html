Wealth inequality: widen productive ownership

Mutual Futures asks whether viable businesses could pass into willing worker and member ownership as owners retire. Community Sovereign Wealth Funds and patient capital could support succession, technology upgrades and operating reserves, while AI-assisted learning builds capability and local returns fund further useful assets.

Mutual Futures connects business succession, shared productive ownership, learning, care and time. A community wealth fund would retain assets and returns; patient finance could support viable transitions without demanding a quick sell-off.

How it could work

  1. Identify viable businesses whose owners want succession and whose workers want a supported ownership pathway.
  2. Publish a financing plan covering purchase debt, wages, reserves, decision rights and losses before a transfer.
  3. Return agreed community-asset income to useful local services or democratically governed investment, with independent accounts.

Everyday example

Workers in a retiring owner's business can buy a share through a viable collective plan instead of losing the business or taking unaffordable personal debt.

Who could act and how it could be funded

States support skills, procurement and regional development; financing, tax and workplace settings need wider coordination. Use independently assessed repayable finance where viable; publish grants and downside exposure.

Fair-go safeguards

No compulsory share purchase or promise of easy returns. Workers keep ordinary employment rights. Community funds need clear custody, conflicts controls and democratic decisions.

What would show progress

Ownership spread; wages; business survival; debt burden; reserves; community returns actually delivered.

What we can check

ABS household income and wealth statistics distinguish flows of income from net worth: assets less liabilities. The detailed 2019-20 distribution is a dated survey, not a 2026 snapshot. Aggregate household wealth can rise while some households remain exposed; averages do not reveal the distribution or who can turn an asset into spendable income.

ABS: income, wealth and survey period

Choices and tradeoffs

Tax and transfer reformCan redistribute resources and fund common services. Design must consider avoidance, incentives, transition, asset-rich cash-poor households and who ultimately bears a tax.
Wider productive ownershipEmployee, cooperative or community stakes can share future returns and voice. They also share risk and require finance, governance and protection against concentrated losses.
Universal services and lower essential costsHousing, health, education and transport can expand real opportunity without everyone owning the same assets. Quality, access and sustainable funding determine the benefit.

A practical public voice

Ask for modelling by wealth group, age, tenure and household type, with debt and regional costs visible. Compare gains in disposable income, service access and productive ownership. A one-year average is not evidence that every generation or household is doing better.

Test a change in productive ownership

Mutual Futures explores business succession into shared ownership, technology upgrades and learning. Its capital and succession workbenches make financing and control assumptions inspectable. A case should show debt, wages, reserves, decision rights and who carries losses; shared ownership does not guarantee returns.
