Public Assets and Long Contracts

Twinkle 01 / Brainstorming scenario

Road tolls: a public deal, a public receipt

Put each toll-road deal on a readable public register, including public contributions, price rises, ownership, expiry and review rights. Use that record to choose the least costly lawful route to fairer travel.

A toll-road contract decides much more than the next price rise. A public register and AI-assisted reading could make the deal understandable enough for people to question it.

How it could work

  1. Transport agencies publish one contract sheet per road, with a plain-English summary beside the source documents.
  2. An independent review compares keeping the deal, renegotiating it and taking public control when available, including compensation and maintenance.
  3. Publish the chosen path, commuter impact and the next review date; track whether promised relief reaches drivers.

Everyday example

A tradie can see why the trip costs what it does, when the concession ends and what buying it back would cost.

Who could act and how it could be funded

States lead concession reviews; the Commonwealth participates where its funding is involved. Fund the register and review within transport administration; any buyback needs its own business case.

Fair-go safeguards

Protect genuine personal information. Commercial secrecy needs a stated reason and review date. Toll relief must not quietly become a permanent subsidy to the operator.

What would show progress

Toll cost per recurring trip; public cost per dollar of relief; contracts readable; review commitments met.

Twinkle 06 / Brainstorming scenario

Power bills: community microgrids and an open energy future

Communities could own microgrids, use AI legal and contract support to negotiate better terms, and reduce demand through efficiency. Load shifting would compare batteries, gravity storage and compressed air. A longer research horizon would examine fission and fusion, keeping future options open rather than betting on one technology.

A microgrid joins local generation, storage and demand. Load shifting saves or moves energy use to a better time. Legal tools, shared ownership and efficiency could make the benefit reach households rather than disappear into another complicated contract.

How it could work

  1. Source-linked AI support could unpack retail, network, connection and finance contracts, while local energy models show household demand and efficiency opportunities.
  2. Community microgrids could combine suitable generation, shared storage, insulation and managed demand. Gravity batteries store energy by lifting a mass; compressed-air systems store it as pressure. Both need a site-specific engineering comparison.
  3. A public research and scenario stream could compare future fission and fusion against other generation and storage paths, including cost, readiness, safety, waste, skills and the rules needed for any deployment.

Everyday example

A town could save daytime surplus for the evening, improve draughty homes and negotiate shared contracts. Renters would have a participation route without buying panels for their landlord.

Who could act and how it could be funded

Community owners, network engineers, councils and governments could assemble projects using member capital, patient finance and public access support. Operating savings, debt and public benefit would have separate accounts. Nuclear research and any legal reform would need their own national and state pathways.

Fair-go safeguards

Choice of storage follows the site and measured demand. Research into fusion or fission is not a promise of available household power. Clear exit, equitable access and human-reviewed contracts protect participants.

What would show progress

Total bills; demand reduced; useful load shifted; losses and maintenance; community ownership; renter access; resilience; future options assessed.

Twinkle 13 / Brainstorming scenario

Resource wealth: show the public return and the influence

Publish a project-level receipt for production, ownership, taxes, royalties, subsidies, cleanup liabilities and local benefit. Connect relevant lobbying and political funding records so people can follow the influence as well as the money.

A resource receipt would follow the full project, including production, ownership, public payments and cleanup. A community wealth fund offers a further question: could some return build assets that stay useful locally?

How it could work

  1. Start with one project and a single reporting year, linking every figure to its original record.
  2. Compare public-return options, supply commitments and cleanup security against total project economics.
  3. Publish meetings, declared conflicts and relevant media ownership links without treating association as proof of wrongdoing.

Everyday example

A town can see what a mine contributes, who carries the rehabilitation risk and which decision-makers met its lobbyists.

Who could act and how it could be funded

States lead resource licensing, royalties and many site obligations; Commonwealth tax and national disclosure reforms need federal action. Fund independent receipt preparation and require proposed fiscal changes to have a published revenue case.

Fair-go safeguards

Separate revenue, profit, royalty and tax. Check beneficial ownership before making claims. Do not assume a low tax figure proves avoidance or that all media coverage is bought.

What would show progress

Complete project receipts; secured cleanup costs; local benefits delivered; understandable public returns; conflicts disclosed.

Plain hook

Tolls should not feel immortal.

The Twinkle version is simple: why do some toll roads never seem to untoll? The policy version is a national public-asset register that tracks long contracts, renewal clauses, public-private partnerships, sale terms, royalties, renegotiation windows and community benefit tests.

Modules

Fair-term leasesCritical infrastructure should have expiry logic, review triggers and public-interest tests, not quiet forever-deals.
Contract sunlightPlain-English summaries should explain price rises, risk transfer, penalties, private returns and public obligations.
Resource returnNatural-resource value should help fund long-term resilience instead of vanishing into short-term budgets.

National/Oceania frame

Australia and our Oceania neighbours need infrastructure that survives shocks: energy, transport, ports, water, telecoms, local compute and food logistics. P4A's first move is not to rip everything up. It is to make the deals legible, compare them fairly, and stop governments signing away public leverage for longer than communities can realistically consent to.

What gets built

A public contract observatory: searchable assets, lease timelines, community benefit dashboards, renegotiation alerts and plain-English legal explainers connected to the Truth Engine.

Resources: public return and affordable supply

DCCEEW’s September 2026 update reports that around three-quarters of Australian gas production is exported. The announced reservation scheme is intended to commence on 1 July 2027, with supply obligations expected from 1 January 2028. The proposed obligation is equivalent to 20% of annual LNG export volumes, adjustable downwards for domestic security needs. Exposure-draft consultation closed on 24 September 2026; current rules remain until new arrangements are implemented.

Gas reform design, dates and status
Reservation and supply obligationsCan secure gas for local contracting, but design affects investment, trade commitments and who qualifies. Reserved volume alone is not a household retail-price guarantee.
Efficiency and electrificationReduce exposure to gas costs where suitable alternatives exist. Upfront investment, renters’ control and industrial process limits must be addressed.
Royalties, tax and public stakesChange the public return from extraction or ownership. Revenue design, risk, investment incentives and cleanup responsibilities differ from the affordability question.

A public-resource record should record production, exports, royalties, PRRT, corporate tax, subsidies, cleanup liabilities and local benefits with the same period and clear entity boundaries. Zero corporate tax is not proof of avoidance. Resource ownership also does not establish Crown surface tenure: land rights, mineral rights, Native Title and approvals need their own records.

Ask your representatives for a project-level receipt and the assumptions behind any claimed bill reduction. Use published gas consultation outcomes and later instruments to follow implementation; the September submission window is closed.
