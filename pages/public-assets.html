Twinkle 01 follow-through

Public Assets and Long Contracts

If a road, port, grid, mine, water asset or data system is essential, the public deserves a clear memory of who controls it, who profits, and when the deal can be reviewed.

Open the roomBack to Twinkle 01Rabbit-hole map

Plain hook

Tolls should not feel immortal.

The Twinkle version is simple: why do some toll roads never seem to untoll? The policy version is a national public-asset register that tracks long contracts, renewal clauses, public-private partnerships, sale terms, royalties, renegotiation windows and community benefit tests.

Modules

Fair-term leasesCritical infrastructure should have expiry logic, review triggers and public-interest tests, not quiet forever-deals.
Contract sunlightPlain-English summaries should explain price rises, risk transfer, penalties, private returns and public obligations.
Resource returnNatural-resource value should help fund long-term resilience instead of vanishing into short-term budgets.

National/Oceania frame

Australia and our Oceania neighbours need infrastructure that survives shocks: energy, transport, ports, water, telecoms, local compute and food logistics. P4A's first move is not to rip everything up. It is to make the deals legible, compare them fairly, and stop governments signing away public leverage for longer than communities can realistically consent to.

What gets built

A public contract observatory: searchable assets, lease timelines, community benefit dashboards, renegotiation alerts and plain-English legal explainers connected to the Truth Engine.

Resources: public return and affordable supply

DCCEEW’s September 2026 update reports that around three-quarters of Australian gas production is exported. The announced reservation scheme is intended to commence on 1 July 2027, with supply obligations expected from 1 January 2028. The proposed obligation is equivalent to 20% of annual LNG export volumes, adjustable downwards for domestic security needs. Exposure-draft consultation closed on 24 September 2026; current rules remain until new arrangements are implemented.

Gas reform design, dates and status
Reservation and supply obligationsCan secure gas for local contracting, but design affects investment, trade commitments and who qualifies. Reserved volume alone is not a household retail-price guarantee.
Efficiency and electrificationReduce exposure to gas costs where suitable alternatives exist. Upfront investment, renters’ control and industrial process limits must be addressed.
Royalties, tax and public stakesChange the public return from extraction or ownership. Revenue design, risk, investment incentives and cleanup responsibilities differ from the affordability question.

public-resource receipt should record production, exports, royalties, PRRT, corporate tax, subsidies, cleanup liabilities and local benefits with the same period and clear entity boundaries. Zero corporate tax is not proof of avoidance. Resource ownership also does not establish Crown surface tenure: land rights, mineral rights, Native Title and approvals need their own records.

Ask your representatives for a project-level receipt and the assumptions behind any claimed bill reduction. Use published gas consultation outcomes and later instruments to follow implementation; the September submission window is closed.

Twinkle 13 — earlier seed and related proposals

A national rant about resources leaving by the shipload with the public return hard to follow, a handful of people getting staggeringly rich off them, and the megaphones that wealth then buys, then a hard turn into every deal public, every tonne counted, and an expiry date on the lot.

Open seed

The everyday gripe

Straight to camera: iron, gas, coal and sand leave by the shipload. Show us the public receipt: production, exports, royalties, taxes, subsidies, cleanup and local benefit. Keep corporate profit and political influence traceable without assuming one tax number tells the whole story. The trapdoor is daylight with a clock on it: publish the terms and review dates. This seed is open: film your own shipload rant.
