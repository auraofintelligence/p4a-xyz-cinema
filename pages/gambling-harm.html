Twinkle 37 / Brainstorming scenario

Gambling: let people watch the game without the sales pitch

Reduce inducements and exposure, make limits work across participating products and fund help independently of gambling promotion. Track the community's full balance sheet.

The gripe is gambling promotion taking over the game and its aftermath. Product rules, advertising, community revenue and useful support belong in the same discussion.

How it could work

  1. Seek enforceable restrictions on gambling promotion around sport and child-accessible settings, with clear responsibility for online and local products.
  2. Develop interoperable pre-commitment and self-exclusion with independent privacy and accessibility review.
  3. Reduce reliance on gambling revenue through a published transition plan, protecting essential community services without preserving harmful marketing.

Everyday example

A family can watch footy without a stream of betting prompts; a person who sets a limit can keep it across participating services.

Who could act and how it could be funded

States can act on gambling products, venues and licensing; national online and broadcast rules need Commonwealth action. Cost enforcement, treatment and replacement service funding openly.

Fair-go safeguards

No public list of debts or gambling history. Limits must not become a commercial profiling tool. Policy evaluates actual harms, including impacts on families, rather than just industry turnover.

What would show progress

Advertising exposure; limits honoured; exclusion failures; harm and support access; public revenue transition.

What we can check

AIHW’s gambling overview draws on different surveys and measures of participation and risk. Those measures are not interchangeable with industry losses, a clinical diagnosis or harm to family members. Compare the population, reference period and method before using a headline percentage.

Choices and tradeoffs

Reduce advertising and inducementsCan lower exposure and pressure, especially around sport. Rules need enforceable definitions and consideration of displaced advertising and sponsorship revenue.
Binding limits and safer product designLoss or time limits and self-exclusion can interrupt harmful patterns. Privacy, evasion, coverage across providers and accessible account recovery matter.
Independent support and revenue reformTreatment, financial counselling and family support need sustained funding. Governments and communities should examine dependence on gambling revenue rather than assume it is free money.

A practical public voice

For help, use Gambling Help Online and BetStop. For policy, ask state and federal representatives which products and advertising they regulate, what outcomes they measure and how revenue conflicts are managed. You can advocate without disclosing debts or a family member’s history.
