Beer tax: the pub pays more than resource rent

The gripe is the imbalance: Australians’ beer attracted more excise and customs receipts than petroleum resource rent tax brought in. A possible fair-go response would strengthen the public return from gas and other resources through better terms, source-linked scrutiny and community wealth mechanisms, rather than make ordinary consumption the easy revenue target.

The joke is about who carries the tax burden and who receives Australia’s resource wealth. Beer receipts and PRRT are different taxes, but their same-year totals make the public-return question hard to ignore.

How it could work

  1. Show the same-year comparison and its limits: the 2024-25 cash receipts were $2.729 billion for beer and $1.420 billion for petroleum resource rent tax. PRRT is one tax, not the gas industry’s total tax contribution.
  2. AI legal and contract tools could trace resource terms, deductions, ownership and public obligations, giving ordinary people a source trail to question who receives the value.
  3. Explore stronger resource-return rules and public or community ownership stakes, with some proceeds held in Community Sovereign Wealth Funds. Any beer-tax reduction would need its own explicit proposed design.

Everyday example

The pub conversation could ask why drinking a schooner is such an easy tax target while the public return from extracting national wealth is harder to follow.

Who could act and how it could be funded

National resource-tax reform needs Commonwealth action; state royalties, project terms and ownership pathways have their own responsibilities. Community wealth structures could retain agreed returns and invest patiently in useful local assets.

Fair-go safeguards

Compare matching years and cash measures; never relabel PRRT as all gas-company taxes. The comparison is a starting point for questioning public returns, not by itself proof of unlawful avoidance.

What would show progress

Public return from resources; terms and deductions traceable; returns retained in communities; household burden; useful assets funded.

The draught freeze is law

The Excise Tariff Amendment (Draught Beer) Act 2026 received assent on 8 April 2026. It sets the indexation factor to one for specified draught-beer rates on 1 August 2025, 1 February 2026, 1 August 2026 and 1 February 2027. The old “every February and August” line is therefore stale for those rates. It does not describe every packaged beer, spirit or wine tax.

The price of a drink has several levers

Targeted hospitality reliefA draught-specific concession can support on-premise trade. Its fiscal cost and actual benefit to venues, staff and customers need measurement.
Review consistency across alcohol taxesCompare beverage categories, alcohol content and administrative complexity. Reform creates winners and losers; transition and health effects need to be explicit.
Fund harm reduction and treatmentServices, prevention and late-night transport address costs not shown on a menu. They need reliable funding and evidence, rather than assuming price policy does every job.

Show the pub receipt

Ask what changed in tax, wholesale costs, rent, wages and the menu price over the same period. Publish the public cost and review date without pretending a venue’s entire bill is tax. Ask representatives how any concession’s pass-through and health effects will be evaluated.

The everyday gripe

Straight to camera, schooner in shot: the draught-beer indexation freeze is now law, but a menu price still has rent, wages, wholesale costs and margins inside it. Meanwhile the money that funds political campaigns surfaces long after the votes are counted. The gag writes itself: this country CAN run things like clockwork, it just chooses which things. This seed is open: film your own price-of-a-schooner history lesson.

Description

BaitThe pub test held in an actual pub. The draught freeze changes the tax story; compare the actual category and date.
SwitchThe gripe is not really the beer. It is that automation and punctuality only ever seem to point one direction: at you.
SystemThe civic ledger: taxes, donations and promises on the same public rail, with dates, categories and corrections visible.
