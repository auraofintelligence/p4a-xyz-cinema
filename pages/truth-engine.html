Political advertising / checked 3 October 2026

Authorised by whom? True according to what?

An election ad can affect your vote, your services and your trust in the result. Knowing its source helps, but does not establish that its claims are true. The AEC explains that federal authorisation requirements identify the source of electoral communication; it does not regulate the political content of those communications. This is the federal position described by the AEC, not a claim that every jurisdiction has identical rules.

AEC: electoral communication and authorisation
Public evidence and correction ledgerAttach sources, dates, disputed interpretations and visible corrections to campaign claims. It is accessible and preserves disagreement, but participation and public attention determine its reach.
Independent rapid adjudicationA review body could assess disputed factual advertising claims during campaigns. Speed matters, but so do independence, clear jurisdiction, adequate resources and a right to answer.
Enforceable truth standardA narrowly defined legal standard could attach consequences to materially false factual advertising. It would need safeguards for opinion, satire, honest mistakes and appeals, and protection against complaints used to silence opponents.

These are options for public debate, not an adopted P4A platform. Truth Engine proposes source trails, claim labels and public repairs; Aura Politics explores a candidate mirror with evidence drills, conflicts disclosure and human appeal. Neither is a claim that software can settle political disagreement.

Save the exact ad, date, platform, authorisation and the specific factual claim. Compare its source with the original document, ask the campaign for evidence or a correction, and publish the response alongside the claim. Use the AEC's authorisation enquiry route for an authorisation issue; a disputed political opinion is a different matter. Ask your representative which truth standard and appeal protections they support.

Real-world precedent

Wales is already testing the line.

The Welsh Senedd debate matters because it shows anti-lying politics is not just a meme. Wales has passed Member Accountability legislation and is actively working through how deliberate deception by elected members should be handled without criminalising honest mistakes, argument, satire or ordinary political disagreement.

P4A's Truth Engine would learn from that: prove intent carefully, separate error from deception, give people a correction path, and make the public record harder to quietly bury.

Pressure. Light. Code. Repeat. becomes a plain-language pattern for the Truth Engine: test under pressure, illuminate assumptions, encode provenance, repeat in public.

Contract standard

The Truth Engine should eventually let ordinary people inspect public claims about tolls, leases, insurance pools, food-security plans and health programs. What contract exists? Who benefits? When does it expire? What evidence supports the reform? What would change, and who checks the model?

Public standard

The promise is not that P4A knows everything. The promise is that important claims should be traceable, uncertainty should be visible, and mistakes should leave a public repair trail instead of disappearing into political fog.

UAP: no giggle tax, a better observation record

NASA’s UAP study asks what data exist, what new observations would help and how reporting can improve. That is a useful public method: preserve time, place, original media, instrument limits and competing explanations. An unresolved observation remains unresolved; neither ridicule nor an invented confirmation adds evidence.

Open archival accessMakes historical reports inspectable, with privacy and security redactions explained.
Better civilian reportingConsistent observation fields improve comparison, but need moderation and protection of witnesses’ identities.
Targeted instrumentationCalibrated observations can test particular questions; equipment and analysis cost money and require a defined research purpose.

An observation dossier can hold the claim, original records, corrections and competing explanations. Search a named archive record, ask for its release status or document a lawful observation without trespassing or exposing private locations. Ancient-history questions have their own room.

The everyday gripe

Straight to camera, esky lid for a lectern: the Americans hold congressional hearings, release the cockpit footage and brief their parliament. Here, the official answer is a polite cough. Whatever is or isn't up there, the gripe is the silence: adults can handle "we don't know". The trapdoor is method for the weird: a standing public channel where a mystery goes in, the evidence gets graded, corrections are public, and nobody pays the giggle tax for asking. This seed is open: film your own "just tell us" rant.

Description

BaitUAP talk is already in every pub, and half the country has watched the released footage. Official silence is the only part that still feels strange.
SwitchFrom "what are they hiding" to "why is there no method": secrecy-by-default is a habit, and it costs trust on every topic, not just this one.
SystemThe truth engine: claim labels, evidence grades, correction paths and public audit trails. Machinery that doesn't flinch at weird is machinery you can trust with everything else.
