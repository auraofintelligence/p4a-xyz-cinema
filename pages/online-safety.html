Twinkle 25 / Brainstorming scenario

Online safety: a human appeal and clear platform responsibility

Require understandable safety decisions, a working complaint route and a human appeal for significant mistakes. Put prevention and response costs on providers as well as expecting users to cope.

A platform’s rules affect livelihoods and daily relationships. A person should be able to understand an action, find a responsible human and contest a mistake.

How it could work

  1. Providers publish plain rules and the reason for account or content action, with accessible ways to contest it.
  2. Track response time for threats, impersonation and repeated abuse, and preserve necessary evidence securely.
  3. Independent reporting shows removals, reversals, unresolved cases and changes to harmful product features.

Everyday example

A small business wrongly locked out can appeal to a human; a person receiving threats gets a clear response pathway.

Who could act and how it could be funded

National platform regulation is central; state services, schools and police have their own support roles. Public contracts include response staffing, accessibility and independent review costs.

Fair-go safeguards

Protection and freedom to contest decisions belong together. Do not equate disagreement with harm. Keep identity checks proportionate and avoid a permanent public register of victims.

What would show progress

Response and appeal time; reversals; repeated failures; harmful design corrected; access to support.

What we can check

eSafety’s Safety by Design approach places responsibility in how services are built and operated. Reporting routes differ by the type of online harm. A platform’s terms, a regulator’s powers and criminal law are separate layers; a removal request is not a general guarantee that every copy will disappear.

Choices and tradeoffs

Safer defaults and less exposureControls over contact, discoverability, location and sharing can prevent harm before a report. Defaults must remain understandable and usable for diverse access needs.
Timely moderation with appealsHuman escalation and transparent decisions can protect people and correct mistakes. Scale, languages, privacy and lawful expression complicate blanket automation.
Minimal-data assurance and accountabilityWhere age or identity checks are justified, compare proportionate methods and retention limits. Collecting more identity data can create a new breach risk.

A practical public voice

Use the service’s safety controls and the relevant eSafety route for a specific incident. Preserve only the evidence the official process asks for; do not repost harmful material. For public policy, compare response times, appeals, recurrence and user control. Children’s needs and AI-enabled financial scams have distinct rooms.
