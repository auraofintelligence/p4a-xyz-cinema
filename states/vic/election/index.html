Keep the first decision simple

Party registration and candidate nomination are separate.

The deadline for a new party application for this election was 1 June 2026. It has passed. Candidate nominations are a different process: they open on 4 November. VEC registration deadline.

Stand as an individual independent

Nominate for one Assembly district or one Council region. Independent nominations need 6 enrolled district nominators for Assembly, or 50 enrolled region nominators per candidate for Council.

Stand with an existing registered party

A registered party can endorse a candidate and lodge their nomination and deposit centrally. Endorsement depends on that party’s own process; appearing on an announcement list is not yet formal nomination.

Form an independent Council group

At least two successfully nominated candidates in the same region can group together, specify their order and share a ballot column and above-the-line box. Independent-only groups cannot request a party group name. Ungrouped candidates appear below the line. VEC group rules.

Build an organisation; register a party later

An unregistered organisation can participate without registered-party ballot benefits. Later registration needs at least 500 eligible members, a constitution, an officer, an application and verification. Votes are not memberships, and winning a seat does not waive registration requirements. Registration process.

Standing: the short practical checklist
  1. Check eligibility, Victorian enrolment and the VEC’s disqualifications. Living in the chosen electorate is not required.
  2. Choose one electorate; complete the form and obtain the required nominator signatures.
  3. Arrange AUD $350 per candidate in cash or bank cheque - $700 for two candidates.
  4. Book and attend the election-office appointment with the form and deposit. Candidate Helper prepares the form; it does not lodge it.

VEC appointments: 4-6 November, 9 am-5 pm; 7 November, 9 am-1 pm. Monday 9 November is reserved for corrections. Any new Monday lodgement needs prior discussion with the election manager. Full eligibility and nomination instructions.

Deposits are refundable if elected, withdrawn in time, or the applicable 4% first-preference condition is met; grouped Council candidates have a group-total provision. Electoral Act, deposit provisions.

After election: affiliation and later party registration

A member changing party affiliation does not, by itself, lose their seat. That does not create a registered party or retrospectively change the ballot. The VEC cannot consider or complete registration between issue and return of the election writ. Registration carries an indexed fee; check the current amount with the VEC rather than relying on its older July 2025 dollar figure. Electoral Act, sections 45-46, 180 and 236 · Current registration guidance.

Money, records and public campaign material

A public candidacy announcement can trigger political-finance duties before nomination. A dedicated state campaign account, disclosure registration and financial records may be required. For 15 April-28 November 2026, the ordinary donor cap is $5,190 per recipient; reportable donations at the $1,290 threshold must be disclosed within 21 days. An approved new entrant may have a $10,380 cap; that higher cap is not automatic.

Donation rules and thresholds · State campaign account · New-entrant rules.

Websites and social posts made by or for candidates or regulated entities can need electoral authorisation. A non-commercial label alone does not decide these obligations. VEC authorisation guidance. This workbench does not collect nominator details, create accounts, accept payments or lodge applications.

How Council voting changes in 2026

Group voting tickets no longer apply. Voters must number at least five boxes above the line or at least five below the line. A five-member region’s quota is the whole number of formal votes divided by six, plus one (roughly 16.7%). Transfers and the full count matter; that is not a guaranteed first-preference winning target.

Representation, without predictions

What would one or two seats mean?

Legislative Assembly

1 or 2 of 88 members

Each district elects one member. The Assembly determines who can command confidence to govern. A majority of all seats is 45; ordinary votes have their own procedural rules.

One or two members could matter to confidence or legislation if the wider result leaves other groups short of support. They do not guarantee a balance-of-power position.

Explore all 88 districts →
Legislative Council

1 or 2 of 40 members

Eight regions elect five members each. Council members scrutinise legislation and government. A majority of all seats is 21; attendance, abstentions and voting rules can affect individual decisions.

One or two members may affect a particular vote when other votes are closely divided. Council seats do not by themselves determine Assembly confidence or who forms government.
