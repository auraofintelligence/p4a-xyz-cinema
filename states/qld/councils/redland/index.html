How the replacement process works
  1. A vacancy, then an election. A mayoral vacancy in the beginning or middle of a council term is filled by a by-election. In the final part it is filled by appointing a sitting councillor. The final part of this term begins on 16 March 2027, the third anniversary of the 2024 election; the 28 October 2026 vacancy is earlier. The deputy mayor acts during the vacancy. Local Government Act 2009, ss 161, 164–165.
  2. Use the official notice. The returning officer fixes the by-election day. The ordinary rule is within two months of the vacancy; the issued notice supplies the actual timetable. The notice sets a nomination day 8–18 calendar days after publication and 18–42 days before polling. Local Government Electoral Act 2011, ss 24–25.
  3. Nominations come before voting. Nominations must reach the returning officer before noon on the official nomination day (s 27). Eligibility, required training, nomination, campaign accounts and disclosure obligations are explained in the ECQ candidate guide. Considering standing, announcing an intention and an accepted nomination are different stages.
  4. Check the result. Follow ECQ’s nomination, voting, count and declaration notices. A countdown reaching its target does not establish that polling occurred or somebody was elected. Council says the elected mayor will serve until the next local elections in 2028.

    Keep three accounts together.

    What it cost

    Money, paid work, materials, assets and continuing obligations. Who pays, and who carries the burden?

    What was given freely

    Chosen time, care, knowledge and resources. Verify contribution with consent; count a human hour once.

    What changed

    Benefits, harm, access, skills, ecology and time. Record what was observed as well as what was hoped for.

    These accounts inform each other. They are not converted into a single score or a fixed dollar value for a C-Hour.
