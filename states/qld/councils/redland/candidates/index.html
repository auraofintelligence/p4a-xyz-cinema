How the replacement process works
  1. A vacancy, then an election. A mayoral vacancy in the beginning or middle of a council term is filled by a by-election. In the final part it is filled by appointing a sitting councillor. The final part of this term begins on 16 March 2027, the third anniversary of the 2024 election; the 28 October 2026 vacancy is earlier. The deputy mayor acts during the vacancy. Local Government Act 2009, ss 161, 164–165.
  2. Wait for the official notice. The returning officer fixes the by-election day. The ordinary rule is within two months of the vacancy; this is not a confirmed event date. The notice sets a nomination day 8–18 calendar days after publication and 18–42 days before polling. Local Government Electoral Act 2011, ss 24–25.
  3. Nominations come before voting. Nominations must reach the returning officer before noon on the official nomination day (s 27). Eligibility, required training, nomination, campaign accounts and disclosure obligations are explained in the ECQ candidate guide. Considering standing, announcing an intention and an accepted nomination are different stages.
  4. Check the result. Follow ECQ’s nomination, voting, count and declaration notices. A countdown reaching its target does not establish that polling occurred or somebody was elected. Council says the elected mayor will serve until the next local elections in 2028.

Following the confirmed mayoral vacancy.

Council has confirmed the vacancy and a by-election is required. Follow the official ECQ notice and current candidate guidance. Community work can also continue outside office.

01

Understand the role

Separate the mayor's responsibilities from decisions requiring council, officers, another government or a service provider. Identify the power and resources needed for each proposal.

02

Consider, stand aside or nominate

Eligibility, training, accounts, expenditure and disclosure obligations need current official guidance. Some obligations can begin before formal nomination. Deciding not to stand is a valid branch.

03

A clear public account

Show proposals, sources, status, costs, volunteered contributions and intended outcomes. Record announcements separately from accepted nominations; any withdrawal follows the applicable official rules.

04

An outcome and a handover

If elected, connect proposals to decisions, budget, delivery, review and the remaining statutory term. A year of service is an illustrative scenario, not a guaranteed term. If not elected, local projects can still continue.
